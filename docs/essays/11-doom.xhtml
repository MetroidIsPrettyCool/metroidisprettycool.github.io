Can’t Pray the Oscillator Frequency Away

Look, Doom is Probably More Demanding Than You Thought it Was

It’s a pet peeve of mine that Doom (1993) gets sooooo much focus in the popular conception of the hacking1 scene (“can it run Doom tho?”), because I honestly don’t think most people have a good grasp of what its hardware requirements actually are.

See, Doom is a complicated program, but more importantly it’s also a complicated game, i.e. interactive software. it doesn’t just have to run, it has to run within a set of real-world constraints, too. Stuff like “don’t take several hours to draw a single frame.”

Id did not set out to make the most portable game ever. They set out to show off the cutting edge of PC gaming in 1993. That meant a 32-bit CPU (80386 or better [you’d want better]), 4 MB of RAM minimum, and graphics hardware that could keep up (VGA or better). Plenty of contemporary devices couldn’t handle that, e.g. the Amiga. Plenty of devices still can’t handle that.

It’s not a question of optimization, or of clever workarounds, or of the author’s technical ability. There is no way optimize “the game needs to draw the level to the screen” faster than the physical limits of the drawing hardware, no workaround for “keep track of the enemies in a level” that does not involve keeping track of the enemies in a level, and no level of technical ability which will let you do more with the system than the silicon is capable of. You cannot pray the oscillator frequency away and just “do it anyway” It’d be like trying to play mail chess on a 4x4 board with no pieces in a country with no mail service. The complexity of the system is the complexity of the system.

Take that headline that was circling around a couple years ago: “This Programmer Figured Out How to Play Doom on a Pregnancy Test”.2 Not true! Foone Turing did not get Doom running on a pregnancy test microcontroller, because that is not physically possible. What did they do? They got Doom running on a 32-bit ARM devkit – think “Game Boy Advance” power levels, both have roughly similar CPUs – that was wired up to the display of a pregnancy test. No shit you can get Doom to run on something with the specs of a GBA, right? There was an official port of Doom the Game Boy Advance!

So why is Doom here? Because after Id open-sourced the engine, it has become a common answer to the question “what interactive graphical application has the most modular codebase?” The actual demo is the LCD driver. Doom’s role here could just as well be filled by a screensaver, or one of those trippy C64 fastload graphics, or an e-book reader.

It’s like watching someone demonstrate a new ACE3 exploit they found, and seeing people come away coming away with the conclusion that the impressive part was “This Programmer Managed to Open The Windows Calculator on Windows 11”. No! That’s not where the effort went!

*​sigh​*.

I guess “Programmer Writes Driver” doesn’t get as many clicks.

Oh well…
