1. General-Topic

1.1. Systems of Measurement

1.1.1. 12-Hour Time or 24-Hour Time?

When I wake up from a nap, I want as little ambiguity possible about whether it’s the afternoon or next morning. 24 hour time!

1.1.2. Best Date Format?

Only ISO 8601 has a unique capability – making lexicographic ordering and temporal ordering the same – every other format is just about personal preference. If you need to standardize on something, then, it makes sense to standardize on the one that’s strictly more useful.

Relatedly, I’m a fan of the so-called “Holocene calendar” or “human era calendar”. Just take the current year, prefix a ’1’, and that gives you roughly (around 300 years off) the number of years since the start of the Holocene, without sacrificing your ability to easily parse out the current Gregorian year. I think that’s a nice little reminder of the dizzying depth of human history. (Or perhaps from a geologic perspective, its dizzying shallowness. Only ten thousand years?)

So: wherever I need a time format string, I pick 1%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S.%N (or 1%Y-%m-%dT%H:%M:%S.%N) – just lop off any precision you don’t need.

1.1.3. Fahrenheit Vs. Celsius?

I am not a bowl of water. I am also not a 17th century thermometer-maker. A scale that is easy to calibrate with pre-industrial tools is not per se any more relevant to me than a scale which is difficult to calibrate with those same tools.

For anthropocentric purposes, “the temperature at which water boils” is probably the least useful point to make an easy-to-remember round number. I already know what temperature water boils at, because I can look at the pot and see it boiling or not. If it is boiling, then it’s at the temperature where water boils. If it isn’t boiling, then it’s less than that. Are there really that many other circumstances where I need to know if something is exactly 212 degrees?

100° should be something useful, but difficult to measure without a thermometer. For anthropocentric purposes, anyway.

(For “doing math with temperatures” purposes, neither Fahrenheit nor Celsius are the right tool. Absolute zero is a clearly more universal constant to fix your zero point to.)

1.1.4. Daylight Savings Time Vs. Standard Time?

People prefer to structure their lives around observable phenomena. (This much should be self-evident to anyone who was doing anything, for any reason, in mid-2020.) In particular, people prefer to be awake – or at least to do their work – when the sun is up. Do we all agree on this? I think we all agree on this.

I think it’s ridiculous to try to force people’s schedules through something so indirect as timezones. It’s like if NHTSA tried to improve driving habits by requiring all speedometers to read 10 km/h faster than actual whenever you were on the highway. You can just wake up earlier! Most of the professions where alignment with sunlight hours really matters (e.g. farming) already do this because it’s obvious. People can regulate their schedules without a shove from the heavy hand of Congress.

Permanent DST is a particularly stupid solution to this problem. If the whole country wanted to wake up an hour earlier, they would do that already. Why do you think we settled on 9-5 and not 8-4? It’s not the time zone. Do you think that everyone in China wakes up at the same time just because their clocks all say “6:00 AM” at the same time? You can’t pray the circadian cycle away.

I guarantee you that we’d all just shift back to 10-6 (indicated), and then the fact that our clocks are misaligned with our neighbors would just end up being a stupid bit of trivia.

2. Technical

2.1. Paradigms

2.1.1. Functional Vs. Oop?

  1. Something something “true OOP has never been tried” something something “actually, I think you’ll find it works really well in Smalltalk/Objective C before it got bad/my custom fork of the Common Lisp interpreter/some other language that, conspicuously, nobody is using to ship real software anymore.”

    Perhaps, among all the many, many languages that call themselves “object-oriented”, there is an example that makes writing good software easy and bad software hard. Myself? I have not found such a language. The state of those that followed Java’s lead is particularly dire…1

    Why must we only encapsulate on object boundaries? Why must we eschew structuring programs as “collections of things to do” for “collections of things we can do stuff to”? Does that not read as inside-out to people?

  2. “Pure functions” are a very elegant idea, on this I agree. I am less sold on the universality of first-order functions. Perhaps it only really shines when you get strict about your principles? I admit, most of my experience with FP has been in multiparadigm languages. Lisp, Rust, etc.

What I really want out of a language is first-class recognition that subprograms are heterogeneous. Some are pure, others stateful; some are sync, some are async; some run on servers, some run on clients; some parts on run CPUs, others on GPUs, or NPUs, or DSPs; some require SIMD, others MIMD; some run natively, others in browsers, others sandboxed, or in bytecode VMs; they interact with hierarchical caches; they interact with branch prediction; they talk to each other, they talk to themselves; and so on, and so on.

I would like a language which recognizes this, and makes it easy to tell the compiler what behavior it should expect of a variable, or a subprogram, or system resource. Not simply the things that it does, but equally importantly the things it doesn’t do. We might then modularize our software on boundaries of shared effects and capabilities, each sub-module further constraining the possibility space until it is trivial to reason about.

The more we can reason, the more powerful the metaprogramming we can provide. Each bottom-level module can settle into its own pseudo-DSL, describing behavior as befits its nature. “I am a collection of pure arithmetic functions on floats, here are some equations, please convert them to optimal SIMD”, “I am a description of a protocol, here is my specification, prove its soundness and provide implementations of the things it describes”, “I am a set of tasks, here is they require, what they do, and what they provide, please construct the backing parallel runtime and data structures to run them in”, “I am a UI, here are my elements and their relationships, please construct an event loop to run them and the means to update my state” and so on and so forth.

Wouldn’t that be wonderful? To be able to imply parallelization, or IPC, or fronted-backend, or coprocessor offload, all with just type signatures? At least for trivial cases?

I understand that there are optimizations compilers will never intuit. A lot of optimizations they’ll never intuit. I hope that, for those cases, this approach would still make it easier to write programs than it would make them harder.

I suspect the answer lies somewhere between LISP, Ada, CUDA, Julia, perhaps OpenMP, functional programming; but I confess I’m too uninformed to say anything definitive. Call me back when I’m more experienced?

2.1.2. Dynamic Vs. Static Typing?

“Check it out guys: we managed to save a whole year building this new airliner by never checking if we bought the right materials!”

I can accept dynamic typing for projects with less than a hundred SLOC. Two hundred is a perhaps, three hundred is really pushing it, and four hundred is way too big to reason about. You’re gonna start making type errors.

If your language has types, I don’t understand why you would want to wait as long as possible to tell me I’ve used them wrong. You aren’t saving me from errors, the error was there to begin with! The language knew that, I didn’t; why hide that?

2.1.3. Strong Vs. Weak Typing?

“Check it out guys: we managed to save a whole year building this new airliner by re-using all these old washing machine bolts! The thread pitches are different, but we just cranked ’em down anyways! They’re almost flush, even!”

Weak typing does not reduce program complexity, it just makes it harder to understand the complexity that was already there. I do not want to add a integer to a string. That is not a well-formed operation. I cannot predict its behavior. I can guess, but I’ll never know for sure, unless I read the standard.

Moreover, when I do read the standard and find therein that String + Integer actually means “convert this integer to a string with .toString() and then .join() them”, then at that point what benefit does this ambiguous + operator actually give me over an explicit String.join(Integer.toString())? Golf cred? Screw golf cred. I want to write what I mean.

2.1.4. Is C Really “Low-Level”?

No,2 and I think that’s holding us back. I shouldn’t have to know how to coerce the compiler into producing the right assembly, I should be able to just say what I expect. Modern ISAs are shockingly expressive, why shouldn’t we let users take advantage of that?

2.2. Hardware

2.2.1. AMD Vs. Intel?

Intel are an amusing company. Their in-house architectures all suck, their CPUs implementing those architectures suck, their CPUs implementing other people’s architectures suck, their attempts to innovate always crash and burn, and somehow they’re still number 1. How does that even happen?

I’d like to see one of their wacky ideas finally pay off one day. ’Til then? Yeah, you should probably just buy AMD…

2.2.2. RISC Vs. CISC?

Any architecture that gives more avenues for static optimization gets my vote. RISC, VLIW – NISC, even – I find these very appealing.

Unfortunately, nobody is really making any new “the ISA is the metal” processors these days. It’s all compromised. Did you know that ARM has a instruction just for JavaScript? FJCVTZS, AKA “Floating-point Javascript convert to signed fixed-point, rounding toward zero”.3

I don’t see the point comparing architectures when the only distinguishing factor is how long or short their microcode listings are. Use whatever gives you the better numbers.

2.3. Ideology

2.3.1. LLMs and “Vibe Coding”?

The most important fact to internalize about large language models is that they’re profoundly stupid. They will lie, they will overstate, they will misunderstand, and they will be biased. There is no question you can ask that will never get a wrong answer. Never do anything with an LLM’s “opinion” that does not centralize this fact.

The second most important fact to internalize is: they cannot tell you if they’re actually confident, or are just answering as if they were confident. On some level they’re the same thing. What’s the last time you asked ChatGPT a question and replied by asking for your help? Has it ever happened? There’s simply no possible way to know if an LLM has a real understanding of a problem if you don’t have that yourself. Never make a decision on the basis of an LLM’s input without considering this too.

Evangelists may attempt to convince you that the solutions to these problems are only a matter of time.

I disagree.

There’s only one real way to get value (and I mean ’value’ here as coldly and pragmatically as possible) out of something that is architecturally incapable of meta-cognition:

  1. Only ever bet your time. If a wrong answer will cost you more than an hour of frustration, reevaluate.
  2. Stay transactional. This isn’t a conversation, you are trying to take advantage of The Man’s several billion ill-spent dollars.
  3. Keep conversations as short as possible and delete them as often as possible, to serve points 1 and 2. If you want a permanent record of what you’ve learned, take notes somewhere else.

If you can’t keep to those rules, try reconsidering what it is you’re trying to do. Break the problem down into smaller parts, where the answers are easy to verify. “How do I make Flappy Bird in Objective-C” will only end badly, “how do I compile Objective-C with gcc” will either be right, or it’ll breadcrumb you to the right answer anyway, or at worst will just fail really obviously.

Don’t be afraid to lead it on, too. If you can’t figure out what library you need to use to draw a 3D graph in python, ask it to write a python program that draws a 3D graph, but don’t copy-paste the code. We’re here for facts. Facts like “what libraries is it using” or “how is it structuring these API calls” or “what does it mention in its docstrings”.

Once you’re done, write down what you’ve learned – or at least take a mental note of what’s happened – then delete the conversation and go again. The less it knows about what you’re doing, the more general its responses will be; and the more general its responses are, the easier it will be verify them.

Eventually, you’ll have a solution and an understanding of the problem. That’s what really matters.

2.3.2. UNIX Philosophy?

Despite what I might have implied earlier, I don’t really care about the UNIX philosophy.

It’s a decent set of principles for writing coreutils, but quickly grow inane when you try to apply them to anything real. What even is “a program”? I’ve never seen a good answer from the diehards that doesn’t contradict what they seem to believe in practice.

Look closer, and you’ll find that most applications are, in fact, collections of smaller programs that all do one thing well.

2.4. Formatting

2.4.1. Tabs vs. Spaces?

Doesn’t matter. Manual indentation is for monkeys on typewriters. As long as you, the maintainer, are willing to put in five minutes to enforce your norms with an .editorconfig,5 I’ll use whatever you want. Otherwise, M-x tabify, M-x untabify, and M-x indent-according-to-mode are my friends.

2.4.2. Significant Whitespace?

Significant whitespace is for idiots who wish they were monkeys on typewriters. I don’t trust a language that incentivizes 1.) never writing an expression longer than my fill-column minus the current indentation level or 2.) looking like Bourne Shell. Worse, it makes tabs vs. space a real nightmare. XML, god bless them, had at least this right: program structure should only ever be determined by the things you can see. Never the things you can’t see.

2.4.3. Brace Style?

Open brace on the same line for blocks where the declaration is a single line, next line iff the declaration contains information that should itself be indented. (i.e. where clauses, throws clauses, other information that goes beyond return type, accessor flags, name, arguments) Braces are indented to the level above their comments (Whitesmiths-style makes my head hurt.) 

int main(int argc, char* argv[]) {
    // do stuff...
}

pub fn flow_inner_wrapper<I, T>(task_context: &TaskContext, tasks: I) -> Result<()>
where
    I: IntoIterator<Item = (&'static str, T)>,
    T: FnOnce(&TaskContext) -> Result<()>,
{
    // do stuff...
}

@Override
public void onLoad(ClassPool pool, String classname)
        throws NotFoundException, CannotCompileException
{
    // do stuff...
}

But in general I’d rather delegate to external tooling than make the decision myself. Configure a formatter hook in your flow orchestration! Or at least make a common configuration for your team’s LSP.

2.4.4. In C: int *foo; or int* foo;?

The latter. I understand the argument for the former, I know that declaring multiple variables on a single line like e.g. int* i, j; means i is an int pointer and j is an int value – I contend that this was a mistake on Dennis Ritchie and ANSI and ISO’s parts. The type is (int*)! Perhaps it was an attempt to make source code take up less disk space, like SGML omissible end tags? It’s still an antiquated mechanism if so.

I get around the problem by just never declaring multiple variables on the same line.

2.5. Other

2.5.1. Editor War?

Emacs forever! (while (and (rules 'emacs) (drools 'vi)) (message "you already know it"))

Vi – and its issue – are text editors. They do many things, and they do not do all of them well. They open files, they insert text into files, they change text in files, they launch subcommands, they run shells, they multiplex, they do IPC, they gesture at extensibility. All in just one program. (Not what you were expecting me to say?)

Emacs is an environment. It is a guest VM with host filesystem access (and remote filesystem access) and ten thousand preinstalled utilities for text manipulation. search-forward-regexp, magit-status, default-indent-new-line, eshell, eww – these are not commands.6 They’re single purpose, well-made, filtering, plain text-acting, inter-operating programs. You can even go look up their source code, whenever you want! M-x find-function RET foo-bar RET.7

Who’s the real UNIX philosophy exemplar here? ed with bolt-ons? Or the purpose-built, hackable, extensible, cohesive software distribution? What do Bash and Vi and tmux and a thousand little ncurses programs – all of which are still complexity you’ll have to learn, I might add – give me that Emacs doesn’t? Worse scripting languages? “Job control”?

No thanks.

3.2.3. Evangelion

  1. So, like, What Is Evangelion Even About?

  3. Is Asuka Just a Bitch?

    No! No, no, no, no, no. Where do you people get these ideas?

    Asuka is a girl who thinks – unconsciously, if not consciously – that the moment she has to depend on someone else – for her self-worth, for love, for a home – they’ll turn on her. Kill her. You know why. How could you blame her?

    She thinks that if she can cultivate and protect an image of her in peoples minds of someone perfect, someone that they need; it will give her room to make up for not being perfect. She’ll sacrifice anything for that. Anything. If her real-world performance doesn’t match her words, then fine, she’ll lie. She’ll lie however big and for however long it takes. If they grow to hate her for it, so be it. If they choose exploit her, so be it. If they deprave and defile her, so be it. It’s worth it, just to know they won’t kill her.

    Compare her with Rei. Rei, who never acknowledges Asuka’s thin pleas for recognition. Rei, the prototype for the dummy plug that killed Toji. The one they picked first. The porcelain doll, willing to kill anyone or be killed by anyone at her master’s orders. That terrifies Asuka! She feels like she has a suicide bomber for a co-worker!

    Shinji isn’t much better. He’s too aloof to openly reciprocate her feelings and that equally frustrates and terrifies her. Stupid Shinji, why can’t he see past her faç​de and hold her? She doesn’t understand it’s a two-way street. Well, besides maybe that bit where he saves her in Magmadiver. There, the deprecation is reversed. He’s stupid for saving someone as worthless as her – the loser.

    The only person Asuka really gels with is her foil: Misato. Oh, Misato. They’re both putting on the same act, more or less. Misato buys Asuka’s BS about being fine so easily because if she didn’t, she’d have to confront her own BS about being fine. It goes by quick, but that other moment in Magmadiver where Asuka barely cracks open the possibility she might be struggling (“You know about my past, don’t you?”) and Misato shuts her down with “that’s ancient history, don’t let it bother you”? That’s the beginning of the end.

    The inevitable tragedy here is twofold:

    First, obviously, she really does need other people. She’s fourteen! When working alone she’s pretty conclusively the worst, least successful pilot out of the first three children; she loves Shinji, and her home life is a choice between “child soldier who lives with her commanding officer”, and hanging up the towel and moving back in with her father and stepmother who… well, for whom moving back in with would be tantamount to suicide.

    Second, her failure to reconcile that drives everyone else to start working to make her replaceable. She just doesn’t know how to “fix” things without sacrificing her relationships. “Maybe if I keep digging this hole deeper”, she thinks, “I’ll eventually dig up a ladder I can use to get out of here.”

    She doesn’t. Cross my heart and hope to die, stick a needle in my eye.

    SEELE are the only people so despicable that they really would kill her just because they can, and they do. You lose! That’s who Asuka is.

    I can watch my $2 thrift store DVDs whenever I want, and they have deleted scenes + BTS content + directors commentary. Even most piracy sites only bother dealing in the movies themselves. I want historical context!

  3. Video Games

    I trust Steam to keep my library playable for my lifetime more than I don’t trust them. I do not extend this trust to anyone else. Physical copies wherever possible.

3.3.3. Is Adblock Piracy?

Probably! But again, who cares? Are you a judge, tasked with interpreting a legal code and deciding whether to sentence someone to prison? No? Then why act like it? Nothing hinges on your opinion.

3.3.4. Is Piracy Theft?

I think there’s an implicit conflation here between “Is piracy moral?” and “Is piracy legal?” So often I see this “piracy isn’t theft” mantra get carted out like a ward against evil against any mention of copyright law. These are unrelated questions!

“Should I commit piracy?” is for you and you alone to decide, “Should I be punished by the legal system for committing piracy” is for the court and the court alone to decide. This is a resource management problem, not a confessional. Choose accordingly.

4. Space and Spaceflight

4.1. Astronomy

4.1.1. Is Pluto Still a Planet?

All models are wrong, but some are irrationally defended by people who wish they were still in the third grade – when “knowing what a rhombus is” was enough to coast through life unalloyed.

2006 was actually the second time we reclassified some former planets, did you know that? That’s “planets” plural; in 1867 1 Ceres, 2 Pallas, 3 Juno, 4 Vesta, 5 Astraea, 6 Hebe and 7 Iris all got reclassified as asteroids (Ceres had been a planet for over 65 years at that point!) and in 2006 134340 Pluto, 136199 Eris, and – guess what – 1 Ceres all got reclassified as dwarf planets. (And there are some other cases of things being called planets for a year or two before getting reclassified, see: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_former_planets)

Ceres is obviously the best dwarf planet, because it’s literally number one!

Not a lot of people arguing that Vesta should be a planet again, right? I mean, maybe they should, but what I mean is that if the argument for Pluto was tradition, you’d think the proponents would be a little choosy with that tradition…

I don’t care how many axes you can drum up that frame things as “rocky planets vs. gas giants” or “it’s about hydrostatic equilibrium” or whatever contrived examples you want to come up with. Yes, there are ways that Pluto is more like some of the eight planets than those planets are like each other. Guess what? There are things to study in astronomy besides mineral geology. The IAU definition of “planet” is more useful in more ways than other definitions. We checked! That was the whole point of making a formal decision! So you aren’t gonna get anywhere with whining. Besides peeving me, anyway.

4.2. Spaceflight History

4.2.1. Space Shuttle Vs. Buran (or) Energia?

It’s easy to argue that Energia-Buran was more capable when it never had to demonstrate any of those proposed capabilities.

It’s a bit… meandering, but see this video:

I also take issue with the position that accepts Buran’s failings but argues for Energia as a standalone heavy launcher. It smacks of apologetics to me. Spacecraft and launch vehicle efficacy is determined by more than just the easy numbers – Isp, cost of propellants, time to prep for launch, etc. It’s very telling that Polyus had to be mounted with its engine upside-down to mate with Energia 6SL. Does this strike confidence in Energia as a general-purpose launch vehicle? That it had such specific requirements for payloads that they might have to be mounted upside-down? That those payloads might have to do a backflip to reach orbit?

STS sucked and killed a lot of people. But I don’t think any other program could have delivered as much as it did, with that budget. Could a next-gen capsule have given us the Hubble Space Telescope? The ISS International Segment? Spacelab? Spacehab? The Long-Duration Exposure Facility? The Solar Maximum Mission rescue? The Wake Shield Facility? EURECA? ATLAS-1 or SEPAC?

I don’t think so.
