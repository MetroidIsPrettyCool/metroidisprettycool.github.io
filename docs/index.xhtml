3. XHTML? In 2025?

Pretty good gag, right? :P

I’m not doing it for compatibility with old browsers (I’m totally fine with using & selectors in my CSS, for example) and I’m not doing it out of any love for XML (it’s an embarrassing format). My reasoning here is totally nontechnical, I just like playing with touys.

  1. HTML is even more embarrassing than XML is, and I want to take whatever tiny stand I can against it.
  2. Org mode1 automatically inserts a link to the W3C Markup Validator at the bottom of the page, and (after a bit of work from me, like writing a post-publishing tidy2 hook) XHTML5 was the easiest to get to pass that validation.3
  3. I like dumpster diving through the annals of technical history to get a better idea of why and how we got here.
  4. XHTML being so strict makes it substantially easier for me to naï​vely script some basic transformations in my export pipeline.

So going mainstream with HTML5 is never gonna happen, it doesn’t meet any of those criteria. A manually assembled XHTML 1.1 + WAI-ARIA 1.0 + IFrames DTD is on the table, though, as a bit of experimental archaeology. Or maybe I’ll go even further back! HTML 3.2, anyone? 2.0? HTML+? I will do it if pushed, don’t test me!

The default Org mode export doctype, interestingly, is actually XHTML 1.0-strict. Boggles the mind they would pair that with the validator link being on by default, when there’s so much hardcoded invalid behavior for that doctype. Style tags in <body>, DPUB-ARIA attributes without the right doctype, all sorts of illegal entities without exposing the configuration options to fix it, etc.
The ARIA is particularly silly. Prioritizing accessibility is admirable, don’t get me wrong, but I don’t understand why you wouldn’t just use https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/Schemata/xhtml-aria-1.dtd if you’re going to bring them in? Or at least bring in the standalone module?
Sure, they aren’t recommended by W3C for real documents; but they also haven’t been touched since 2010, and the DTD traffic problem isn’t exactly pertinent anymore. (Moreover: if you cared so much, you could also host them yourself!)
